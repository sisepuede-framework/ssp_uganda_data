# Proposed changes to the Uganda climate chatbot

**Version 2 — for expert review · 29 July 2026**
Prepared in response to comments from David Groves and Edmundo (24 July 2026).

---

## 1. Purpose and how to review this

Two reviewers looked at the tool before it is shared more widely. Their comments fall into three
groups:

1. **The tool's vocabulary is misleading.** It was called a *Simulator* and described every answer as
   a *simulation*, when only six answers are real simulations from Uganda's Country Development
   Pathway Model (CDPM). Everything else is estimated by a metamodel. A World Bank reviewer reading
   "simulation" over an estimate would be right to object.
2. **The novelty is invisible.** What is new is that the six official pathways — previously readable
   only in a document — can now be interrogated in conversation *and* extended to combinations that
   were never officially simulated. Nothing on the screen said so.
3. **Users do not know what they can ask**, so they do not engage.

This document records every change made in response. Sections 2–7 are **implemented** and can be
checked in the running tool — section 7 is the new three-tab layout, added on 29 July. Section 8 is
**open** and needs the experts: it asks what the six pathways should be called and why two of them
behave the way they do.

**How to comment:** use Word's comment tool directly on the text. There is no comment table here on
purpose. The definitions of the levers and uncertainties themselves are in a companion document,
*What the Explorer knows: RDM, the L's and the X's* — that is where lever wording should be corrected.

## 2. Change 1 — The name

**Uganda Climate Policy Simulator → Uganda Climate Pathways Explorer**

David's reasoning, in his words:

> "I would favor *Explorer* over *Simulator*, because some responses retrieve results from official
> simulations while others are estimates generated by the metamodel."

He also noted a concern about the word *Policy*: "you aren't exploring Policies, but Pathways." Both
points are addressed by the new name, which drops *Policy* and *Simulator* together.

**Alternatives considered and not taken:** *Uganda Low-Emission Pathways Explorer* (narrows the tool
to emissions when it also reports costs and development benefits); *Uganda Climate and Development
Pathways Simulator* (keeps "Simulator"); *Uganda Transition Pathways Explorer* (less legible to a
client or World Bank reader than "Climate").

The rename is stated on screen — the header carries the line *"Formerly the Uganda Climate Policy
Simulator"* — so reviewers who saw the earlier version are not confused.

Where the name was changed: the browser tab title, the on-screen header, the API service name, the
server startup banner, and all internal documentation.

## 3. Change 2 — The vocabulary rule

One rule now governs every piece of text in the tool, including the words the assistant itself uses:

| Never say | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Simulator" | **Uganda Climate Pathways Explorer** | Only six answers are simulations |
| "simulation" (for any answer) | **official pathway result** — for the 6 stored CDPM runs<br>**metamodel estimate** — for everything else | The reviewer's central point |
| "Quick start" | **Official pathways** | Names what the buttons actually return |
| "infinite" combinations | **a very large range of pathway combinations** | "Unless the combinations are literally unbounded… more accurate and defensible" (David) |
| "HBLE" unexplained | **High Benefits, Low Emission (HBLE) pathway**, the analytical basis for Uganda's **NDC 3.0** | Required for the client |
| "the model" / "the AI" | Uganda's **Country Development Pathway Model (CDPM)**, developed with the **SISEPUEDE** framework | Correct attribution |
| "co-benefits" (in prose) | **development benefits** | Plainer for a policy audience |

This rule was applied both to the screen and to the assistant's own instructions. That second part
matters: had we only changed the buttons, the assistant would have kept writing "this simulation
shows…" underneath them.

## 4. Change 3 — Every wording change

### 4.1 The opening text

**Before:**

> Welcome. This tool simulates how Uganda's climate policy choices affect greenhouse gas emissions,
> implementation costs, and co-benefits between 2025 and 2070.
>
> Each simulation compares your scenario against Business as Usual and the HBLE pathway. Try asking:
>
> - "What happens if Uganda follows the HBLE pathway by 2070?"
> - "What if Uganda fully decarbonizes its electricity grid and transport sector?"
> - "How much do emissions change if deforestation is halted and forests are restored?"
> - "What if Uganda's GDP grows faster than expected — how does that affect emissions?"
>
> Or use the scenario buttons above to run a preset simulation.

**After** (David's compact version, with the training-run count filled in):

> **Welcome to the Uganda Climate Pathways Explorer.** This tool examines how different levels of
> sectoral transition and alternative country assumptions could affect Uganda's emissions, costs, and
> development benefits between 2025 and 2070.
>
> The pathway buttons above show results from official simulations produced by Uganda's **Country
> Development Pathway Model (CDPM)** using the **SISEPUEDE** framework. These include the **High
> Benefits, Low Emission (HBLE) pathway**, which provides the analytical basis for Uganda's NDC 3.0.
>
> Questions that depart from the official pathways are estimated using a live **metamodel** trained on
> ~99,000 CDPM runs. The metamodel can explore a very large range of transition ambitions and
> assumptions — such as GDP growth or implementation pace — but provides less granular results than
> the official simulations.
>
> Results are compared with Business as Usual and the HBLE pathway. Select a pathway above or ask,
> for example:
>
> - "How does the HBLE pathway differ from Business as Usual?"
> - "What happens if Uganda reaches only part of the HBLE ambition in transport and electricity?"
> - "How would faster GDP growth affect emissions and implementation costs?"
> - "What are the effects of delaying the transition in one or more sectors?"
> - "Which sectoral transitions contribute most to emissions reductions and development benefits?"
>
> Not sure what you can change? Ask *"what can I ask you?"* — the tool will list the sectoral
> transitions and country conditions it can vary.

The five example questions are David's, unchanged.

*Superseded on 29 July:* the three-tab layout in section 7 replaced this single welcome block with a
short intro per tab, and moved the example questions onto the tabs they belong to. The wording above
survives in the tab intros; the vocabulary is unchanged.

**[TO CONFIRM]** The figure **~99,000 CDPM runs** is what the system currently asserts. Someone
should verify it against the training table before this text goes to World Bank reviewers.

## 5. Change 4 — What the tool now says about itself

The assistant's own instructions were rewritten, because the vocabulary rule is worthless if the
answers contradict the buttons. Three additions matter for review:

**It introduces itself differently.** It now describes the analysis as resting on Uganda's Country
Development Pathway Model, developed with SISEPUEDE, and states plainly that it answers from two
sources: official pathway results, and metamodel estimates of combinations that were never officially
simulated. It is explicitly told that this combination is the point of the tool.

**Every estimate is labelled in its first sentence.** An answer produced by the metamodel must open by
saying so ("The metamodel estimates that…"). An answer from a stored run must name itself an official
pathway result. There is no longer a wording in which the two can be confused.

**It can now answer "what can I ask you?"** Previously this got a vague reply. It now answers
concretely, in three parts: the six official pathways; the **54 sectoral-transition levers across 16
sectors** (Transportation 11, Solid Waste 7, Agriculture 4, Buildings & Other Combustion 4, Land Use
4, Livestock Manure Management 4, Electricity 3, Industrial Processes 3, Water & Wastewater Treatment
3, Cross-Sector 2, Industrial Energy 2, Livestock 2, Wastewater 2, Forestry 1, Fugitive Emissions 1,
Transport Demand 1); and the **13 country conditions** (GDP growth, population growth, fossil fuel
prices, agricultural export volumes, four technology capital costs, and five demand elasticities). It
then states what is out of scope and invites a concrete question.

That list is generated from the model's own feature registry, so it cannot drift from the levers that
actually exist.

**One error was corrected along the way.** The assistant had been told that "what if GDP is higher"
should adjust group 62. Group 62 is *Battery Storage Capital Cost*; GDP Growth Trajectory is group 57.
The instruction now points at 57.

## 6. Change 5 — Layout options *(open — your decision)*

Edmundo's comment:

> "Rather than having that at the top, introduce the tool first, then have a few mini sections with a
> set of buttons that a user could just click through to explore the results… The first set would just
> be the existing buttons (clarifying what HBLE is). But then have buttons that include prompts about
> different assumptions of ambition (varying the Ls)… Then have another set of buttons suggesting
> questions related to Xs… Can these buttons live in a side bar that are always visible — say, to the
> left of the chat window?"

Three layouts have been specified for prototyping. Screenshots go below once Claude Design returns
them.

### Option 1 — Persistent left rail
Introduction at the top of the chat; an always-visible left sidebar with three collapsible groups of
clickable prompts: **Official pathways**, **Ambition — the sectoral transitions (L)**, **Conditions —
the country assumptions (X)**. This is Edmundo's request taken literally.

*Solves:* discoverability, permanently. *Costs:* narrows the chat column; the sidebar must collapse
on small screens.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Option 1]**

### Option 2 — Guided landing, then chat
A full-width introduction screen first (what the tool is, the two answer types side by side, a small
XLRM diagram, a short walkthrough), collapsing into Option 1's rail once the user asks anything.

*Solves:* the first-time World Bank reviewer who has never seen the tool. *Costs:* an extra click for
returning users.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Option 2]**

### Option 3 — Tabbed workspace
Tabs across the top — Explore · Official pathways · Ambition · Conditions · How this works — with the
chat persistent below.

*Solves:* room to grow (documentation, the lever catalogue, a tutorial). *Costs:* content behind tabs
is content users may never open.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Option 3]**

**Decision: Option 3 — tabbed workspace, chosen 29 July 2026.** Built and running; see section 7 for
what it actually looks like.

Whichever is chosen, these are kept unchanged: the **"How I got this answer"** panel (both reviewers
singled it out), inline charts placed mid-answer, the official-vs-estimate badge, and the BAU and
HBLE reference lines on every chart.

## 7. The new layout, as built

Three tabs replace the single strip of buttons. The conversation is shared across all of them —
switching tabs never loses your place — and each tab states plainly where its answers come from.
Once you ask something, the panel folds away so the charts and **"How I got this answer"** have the
full height; the tab bar stays, and one click brings the panel back.

### 7.1 Tab 1 — Official pathways *(opens here)*

Six cards, one per official pathway: name, a one-line description, the emissions trajectory as a
small chart, and **2070 net emissions with % vs BAU**. Clicking a card opens that pathway in the
conversation below. Underneath, three questions that span all six at once.

Every figure on these cards is read from the same stored CDPM run the card opens, so a card can never
show a number that disagrees with the answer behind it. Nothing here is estimated.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Tab 1 Official pathways]**

### 7.2 Tab 2 — Explore

Two columns that mirror the structure of the analysis: **Ambition — the sectoral transitions**
(54 levers across 16 sectors — what Uganda decides) and **Conditions — the country assumptions**
(13 uncertainties — what the world decides). Each column carries three ready-made questions from the
reviewers' suggestions, plus chips naming the real sectors and conditions, with their real counts.
The panel states up front that answers here are metamodel estimates of combinations that were never
officially simulated.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Tab 2 Explore]**

### 7.3 Tab 3 — How this works

This tab is a **reference document**, not a launcher, and it looks like one: no chat, no collapse
control, plain paper rather than the panel tint, and a section list down the left for jumping between
parts. It carries the two kinds of answer with the same badges used on the answers themselves; a short
explanation of Robust Decision Making and the L / X split; every one of the 54 levers grouped into its
16 sectors, with a filter box over them; all 13 conditions; and what the tool reports and cannot answer.

The lever and condition lists are generated from the model's own feature registry, so this page can
never drift from what the tool actually knows.

**Asking from the reference.** Every lever, sector and condition carries a small **Ask** button.
Pressing it composes the question, switches to **Explore**, and the answer lands in the same single
conversation as everything else. We considered giving this tab its own separate conversation and
decided against it: two identical-looking threads means an answer can vanish from view with no way to
tell where it went, and it breaks the follow-up — read that transport has 11 levers, ask what they do,
then ask what happens if you push them all. The tab switch is the feedback: it shows you where answers
live, every time.

> **[SCREENSHOT — Tab 3 How this works]**

### 7.4 Wording changed by the new layout

| Where | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Header sub-line | Formerly the Uganda Climate Policy Simulator | Country Development Pathway Model · SISEPUEDE — formerly the Uganda Climate Policy Simulator |
| Pathway launcher | A single row: "Official pathways:" + six buttons | Tab 1, with six described cards carrying real 2070 figures |
| Welcome message | One block of text for everyone | A short intro per tab, replaced by the conversation once you ask something |
| Input placeholder | Ask about an official pathway, or describe a combination to explore… | Changes per tab — e.g. "Describe a combination to explore — an ambition level, a delay, a country condition…" |
| Discoverability | The line "Not sure what you can change? Ask 'what can I ask you?'" | The full catalogue on tab 3, plus sector and condition chips on tab 2 (the chatbot still answers the question too) |

### 7.5 One thing to note about the numbers

Read across the cards and **NDC 2.0 ends lower in 2070 (97.9 Mt) than NDC 2.5 (125.8 Mt)** — the
higher-numbered pathway is the higher-emitting one at the end of the horizon. That is what the stored
runs contain; it is not a display error. It will be the first thing a reviewer asks about, so it
belongs with the other pathway questions in section 8.

## 8. Open proposal — renaming the pathways themselves *(needs the experts)*

Separate from the tool's name, and not yet decided. The six pathways are currently labelled:

| Shown today | SISEPUEDE strategy code | Proposed label *(draft)* | Question for the team |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAU | `BASE` | Business as Usual (BAU) | Spell it out on first use? |
| NDC 2.0 | `PFLO:NDC_2` | NDC 2.0 | Which submission/vintage does this correspond to — should the label say so? |
| NDC 2.5 | `PFLO:NDC_25` | NDC 2.5 | Same question |
| NDC2 Unconditional | `PFLO:NDC_25_UNCONDITIONAL` | NDC 2.5 — Unconditional | The code says **2.5**, the label says **2**. Which is right? |
| NDC2 Uncond. (Alt) | `PFLO:NDC_25_UNCONDITIONAL_CE` | NDC 2.5 — Unconditional (`_CE` variant) | What does "CE" stand for, and what distinguishes it? "(Alt)" tells a reader nothing. |
| Candidate NDC3 / HBLE | `PFLO:HBLE` | **NDC 3.0 (HBLE)** | One pathway currently carries two names used interchangeably. Should NDC 3.0 lead, with HBLE in parentheses? |

The card descriptions now shown on tab 1 are drafts written to be modelling-factual — they say what
each pathway is in the model and nothing more. They need your sign-off, or replacement.

Three problems worth settling:

0. **NDC 2.0 ends below NDC 2.5 in 2070** (97.9 vs 125.8 Mt CO₂e, both from the stored runs). Is that
   expected — different measure coverage, a different ramp — or does one of these runs need
   revisiting? It is visible on the cards, so reviewers will see it.


1. **One pathway, two names.** *HBLE* and *Candidate NDC3* refer to the same run. Users see both. If
   this pathway is the basis for NDC 3.0, leading with **NDC 3.0 (HBLE)** would make that visible
   everywhere it appears.
2. **Labels that do not describe anything.** "(Alt)" and the 2 / 2.5 mismatch above need an
   authoritative answer from whoever defined the strategies.

**Note on implementation:** the pathway names are also how the assistant recognises a request. When
the labels change, the old names must be retained as recognised aliases, or asking for "HBLE" will
stop working. This is straightforward but must be done deliberately.
