# E3 results: what makes the agent stop before the answer, and what helps

Run 2026-11-17 (04:33–04:53 UTC), 393 of 393 conversations, no infrastructure faults. Design frozen at `3a66a2d`
(tag `exp/E3-frozen`, `docs/run_design_E3_2026-11-17.md`); every log records that commit. Scored with
`failure_modes --reader`; manifests written and verified (`backend/rdm/eval/run_manifests/e3_*.csv`). Analysis fixed
before the run: `backend/rdm/eval/analyze_e3.py` → `results_analysis/e3_analysis.xlsx`. Registry IDs E3a, E3b, E3c.

**Status: results before the hand check.** "Valid" is a text rule (a value with the asked unit inside the key); every
match is listed in the workbook sheet "Conversations (hand check)" for Alexa to confirm. Several answers list values
for more than one lever third; the rule counts the answer valid if any value is inside the key (as pre-registered).

Arms: **P** Map+ (control) · **H** calibration hidden at the lever · **L** role sentence in the lever's reply ·
**T** the same sentence in the tool description. Tests: arm labels permuted within question, two-sided.

## What the results say

1. **The number at the lever is the cause (E3a).** Hiding the lever's calibration, with everything else equal,
   raised the share of conversations that reached the answer from 15% to 73% (9 → 44 of 60), and valid answers from
   8 to 44. It holds on the six new targets (6 → 19 of 30), so it is not specific to the questions where the pattern
   was found.
2. **It is not only the share-type questions.** Hiding the calibration also helped on the other-type questions
   (8 → 18 of 24), where the lever's input does not look like the answer. And in this run P reached the answer on
   other-type questions less often than Map+ did in E1/E2 (8 of 24 vs 17 of 25 on similar targets; 3 vs 5
   repetitions). So the share-type account from A4 is too narrow: the calibration numbers at the lever lead the agent
   to stop in two ways, by answering with the lever's share and by concluding the quantity is unavailable.
3. **One sentence about the lever's role helps, but much less than removing the number (E3b).** Valid answers went
   from 8 to 18 of 60 with the sentence in the reply (L) and in the instructions (T). **Where the sentence goes made no
   difference** (18 vs 18), although the test could only have detected a large difference. The sentence reduced "said
   unavailable" (20 → 12–14) but **not** answering with the share (20 → 23 and 20). On the new targets alone its effect
   was not significant (6 → 9 of 30).
4. **Naming the unit removes the share answer but not the stop (E3c).** With "how many tonnes", the agent almost
   stopped giving the lever's share (20 → 4) and valid answers doubled (8 → 17), but many of those conversations
   ended with "the tools cannot give this" instead (20 → 30 said unavailable).
5. **Translation is safe** with the sentence in either place (off the key 1–2 of 15).
6. **The two forms of stopping need different fixes:** the explicit unit fixes the share substitution; the role
   sentence partly fixes "said unavailable"; only removing the calibration from the lever fixes both. But H removes
   what translation needs (smoke test P6: no calibration, no policy run). **Design implication (to test, not tested
   here):** keep the calibration available for translating the user's words, but out of the retrieval path, for
   example as a separate question to the network ("what does this lever level mean?") rather than in every lever
   reply; and state the target quantity and unit before searching.

## Caveats

- Hand check pending (valid answers and stop codes); stop codes are text rules on the opening of the final answer.
- The share-type labels are judgements fixed before the run; result 2 suggests they matter less than assumed.
- 12 share-type targets, two pairs sharing a lever (results hold without the second of each pair).
- O is a descriptive check (8 × 3); its P rate differs from E1/E2 on similar targets, so either the agent or the new
  general wordings (S35 "how far") changed something; the concurrent P control makes the within-E3 comparisons valid.
- One agent model (Claude Sonnet 5.5, medium effort), one model (SISEPUEDE), scripted users.
