# E4 results: calibration on request works only together with a stated target

Run 2026-11-17 (15:20–16:05 UTC), 945 of 945 conversations, no infrastructure faults, agent cost $39.8. Design frozen
at `b032692` (tag `exp/E4-frozen`, `docs/run_design_E4_2026-11-17.md`); every log records that commit. Scored with
`failure_modes --reader`; manifests written and verified. Analysis fixed before the run: `backend/rdm/eval/analyze_e4.py`
→ `results_analysis/e4_analysis.xlsx`. Registry IDs E4a, E4b.

**Status: results before the hand check** (sheet "Conversations (hand check)"). "Valid" is a text rule (a value with
the asked unit inside the key).

Arms: **P** Map+ (control) · **H** calibration hidden, not available · **R** calibration on request
(`meaning=true`) · **F** stated target (`looking_for` in every call), calibration shown · **C** both. All run together.

## Primary: set S, 12 share-type questions × 10 (Holm-adjusted over six contrasts)

| # | Contrast | What it isolates | Valid | p (Holm) | Reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | R vs P | calibration on request, no target | 17 vs 15 of 120 | 0.80 | 18 vs 16 |
| 2 | C vs F | calibration on request, with target | **61 vs 33** | < 0.001 | 73 vs 34 |
| 3 | F vs P | target, calibration shown | **33 vs 15** | < 0.001 | 34 vs 16 |
| 4 | C vs R | target, calibration on request | **61 vs 17** | < 0.001 | 73 vs 18 |
| 5 | C vs P | the whole design | **61 vs 15** | < 0.001 | 73 vs 16 |
| 6 | R vs H | on request vs not available | 17 vs **90** | < 0.001 | 18 vs 92 |

- **Interaction (estimate):** the target raised valid answers by 0.37 with the calibration on request (C − R), and by
  0.15 with it shown (F − P): +0.22. The workbook labels this row "does the target help more when the calibration is
  shown?". The computed difference is (C − R) − (F − P), so a **positive** value means the target helps more when
  the calibration is **on request** (labelling slip, see deviations).
- **Not a pre-registered contrast:** C 61 vs H 90 of 120. C recovers about 60% of the gain of hiding the
  calibration ((61 − 15) / (90 − 15)).

## How the agent used the two changes (sets S and O)

- **Meaning:** in R and C the agent asked for the lever's meaning in **every** conversation (144 of 144 each), always
  before walking. On these questions it needs a translation too ("as ambitious as realistically possible").
- **`looking_for`:** it named a unit in 73 of 120 F conversations and 55 of 120 C conversations on S.

## What the results say

1. **Moving the calibration out of the lever's reply does nothing by itself (R = P).** The agent asks for it at once,
   in every conversation, because it needs it to translate the question. It then stops as it does with Map+: it
   answers with the lever's share (52) or says the quantity is unavailable (30). What stops the agent is not that the
   number is shown unasked, but that it has the number when it starts looking.
2. **Stating the target helps on its own (F: 15 → 33)**, mainly by reducing the share answer (61 → 34). It does not
   reduce "said unavailable" (29 → 32), as in E3's explicit wording.
3. **The two together work much better than either (C: 61 of 120, four times P).** C reduces both forms of stopping:
   - the share answer, 61 → 25;
   - "said unavailable", 29 → 9.

   The target helps more when the calibration is on request (+0.37) than when it is shown (+0.15). A plausible
   reading is that in C the calibration comes back in a separate reply labelled as the lever's meaning, while the
   stated target sits at the top of every reply.
4. **Translation is kept**: R, F and C are as good as Map+ (3–5 of 45 off the key). **H loses it** (31 of 45).
5. **Hiding the calibration still retrieves best (H 90 vs C 61), but it cannot translate.** Among the designs that
   both translate and retrieve, C is the best tested. It closes about 60% of the gap between Map+ and H.

## Caveats

- **Hand check pending.** 12 C conversations reached the answer node but are not valid: N4 87–90 kt (key p90 85 kt)
  and N5 3.5–3.6 Mt (key p90 3.56 Mt). These are the agent's "own scaling" estimates just above the key, and are
  worth a look.
- **S3 was never valid in any arm** (0 of 50 per arm, including H), and N3 and N6 only in H. These questions may be
  hard for reasons other than the calibration.
- **O is descriptive** (24 per arm): no contrast is significant there.
- One agent model (Claude Sonnet 5.5, medium effort), one simulation model (SISEPUEDE), scripted users.
